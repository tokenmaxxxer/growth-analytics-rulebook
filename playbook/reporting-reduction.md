# Report-reduction (subtraction) rules

1. **When** drafting a funnel-diagnosis or experiment-trust deliverable,
   **actively look for a subtractive fix (remove a step, remove a
   confusing field, remove a metric) before defaulting to an additive
   one** — people systematically default to additive solutions and
   under-search subtractive ones even when subtraction is the better fix
   and is not more effortful to identify, only less likely to be
   generated without deliberate cueing.
   Source: Adams, Converse, Hales & Klotz, "People systematically
   overlook subtractive changes," *Nature* 592 (2021), pp. 258–261, DOI
   10.1038/s41586-021-03380-y — summary confirmed via
   https://phys.org/news/2021-04-brains-opportunities.html (fetched
   2026-08-13).

2. **REMOVAL** — when a funnel-diagnosis section has more than one
   prioritized recommendation, **cut every recommendation but the one
   scoped to the single weakest stage**; a multi-recommendation report
   forces the reader to re-derive the priority order the analyst was
   supposed to already have made, adding cognitive load rather than
   reducing it.
   Source: Adams, Converse, Hales & Klotz, *Nature* 592 (2021) —
   cognitive-load-of-unreduced-options implication, same citation as
   above.
